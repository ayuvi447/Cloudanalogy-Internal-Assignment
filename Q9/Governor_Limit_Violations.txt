Governor-Limit Violations and Anti-Patterns in the Broken Code

1. SOQL Query Inside Loop (Contacts)
   Problem: A SOQL query runs once per Account record.
   Why Wrong: Can easily exceed the 100 SOQL queries per transaction limit.

2. DML Update Inside Loop (Contacts)
   Problem: Each Contact is updated individually inside a loop.
   Why Wrong: Can exceed the 150 DML statements limit and hurts performance.

3. SOQL Query Inside Loop (Opportunities)
   Problem: Opportunity records are queried separately for every Account.
   Why Wrong: Causes excessive SOQL usage and poor scalability.

4. DML Insert Inside Loop (Opportunities)
   Problem: Opportunities are inserted one-by-one.
   Why Wrong: Consumes unnecessary DML statements and reduces performance.

5. SOQL Query Inside Loop (Account Re-query)
   Problem: The Account is queried again even though it is already available in Trigger.new.
   Why Wrong: Wastes SOQL queries and CPU time.

6. Updating Trigger Records in an After Trigger
   Problem: The code performs update acc inside an after trigger.
   Why Wrong: Causes extra DML operations and can create recursion issues.

7. No Bulkification
   Problem: Logic processes records one at a time instead of as collections.
   Why Wrong: Fails when large data volumes are processed.

8. Excessive CPU Consumption
   Problem: Multiple nested loops, repeated queries, and repeated DML operations.
   Why Wrong: Can lead to CPU time limit exceeded errors.

9. Re-querying Existing Data
   Problem: Account data is queried even though it already exists in Trigger.new.
   Why Wrong: Unnecessary database calls waste governor limits.

10. Trigger Contains Business Logic
    Problem: All logic is written directly in the trigger.
    Why Wrong: Makes maintenance difficult and violates trigger-handler best practices.

11. No Collection-Based DML
    Problem: Records are not accumulated into lists for a single DML operation.
    Why Wrong: Creates excessive database operations.

12. Poor Scalability
    Problem: The design assumes a small dataset.
    Why Wrong: Breaks when processing hundreds or thousands of records.

13. Potential Trigger Recursion Risk
    Problem: Updating Account records from an Account trigger can re-fire the trigger.
    Why Wrong: Can lead to infinite loops and unexpected behavior.

14. No Separation of Concerns
    Problem: Trigger, querying, business logic, and DML are mixed together.
    Why Wrong: Makes testing, debugging, and maintenance harder.
